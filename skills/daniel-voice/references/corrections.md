# Corrections log

Append-only. Every rule Daniel has stated directly, with his reasoning and the
sentence that triggered it. The failing example teaches faster than the rule,

Newest entries at the bottom.

### 2026-08-27 — Em dashes

**Rule:** Zero by default. One per piece at most, only if genuinely unavoidable.

**His reasoning:** some teachers read the em dash as a sign that AI wrote the
piece. Defensive, not aesthetic.

**Context:** the 1.x skill called this a hard rule with no reason attached,
which broke on contact with his own corpus, since his finished work uses them.
Stating the reason makes the rule survive that contradiction.

### 2026-08-27 — No `and`, `that`, or `but` as openers

**Rule:** no sentence, clause, or phrase begins with these.

**Triggering draft:** `Coco learns this before she learns anything else, and
she learns it by accident, because she copies a design she does not understand
and it works anyway. That is the part I keep returning to.`

**Note:** he called it weak writing. Sentence-initial `and`/`but` is permitted
by usage authorities, so do not defend the rule on grammar grounds. The
underlying problem in that draft was real: clauses chained with `and...and...
because` until the sentence had no shape, then a demonstrative pointing at no
specific noun.

### 2026-08-27 — `and so`, never bare `so`

**Rule:** `and so` is the causal connective. Bare `so` only in rare cases.

**Evidence:** bare `so` as a clause opener appears nowhere in the corpus.
`and so` appears in the RFK piece, reflection 2-5, Gray Affection, and the
Esther form.

### 2026-08-27 — Flow above everything

**His words:** the thing AI models are really bad at is establishing smoothness
or flow.

**Diagnosis:** drafts were built sentence by sentence to display patterns from
the corpus. Nothing carried between them. Flow comes from each sentence opening
on what the last one established.

### 2026-08-27 — Chopped structure

**Rule:** do not repeat `independent clause, and independent clause` down a
paragraph. Vary the joining structure.

**Triggering draft:** six consecutive sentences all built that way. He said he
genuinely cannot stand chopped structure.

### 2026-08-27 — Sentence weighting

**His question:** do you want to weight `Buntarō climbs alone` or `Nitta never
settles the question`?

**Answer adopted:** Gopen and Swan. Topic position carries old information,
stress position carries the payload. Decide what the sentence is about and what
is new about it before building it.

---

### 2026-08-27 — Punctuation is checked, not trusted

**Errors he caught:** missing comma after an introductory phrase (twice, in
separate drafts), missing comma before `and` joining independent clauses, a
wrong comma before an essential `because`, and a verb split from its complement
by an aside.

**Fix:** these are now a separate proofreading pass rather than rules trusted
to hold during drafting.

---

### 2026-08-27 — Stuffing

**His words:** you're stuffing some sentences together when they shouldn't be.

**Diagnosis:** overcorrecting the chopping problem by subordinating whatever
made a sentence longer. LL1 gives the rule: the payload belongs in the main
clause. LL2 gives the decision procedure: subordinate only what is genuinely
secondary.

---

### 2026-08-27 — AI verb constructions

**His example:** `his conclusion holds`, which he called not natural at all.

**Pattern:** an abstract noun handed a verb no person would use about it. If
the subject cannot act, the verb is covering for it.
